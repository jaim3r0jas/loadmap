A diagram the reader pans and zooms. `<Loadmap zoom>` (and `useLoadmap`, `<LoadmapSource>`) puts the zoom buttons on the board (− · the zoom · + · Fit): a drag pans, with a hand to hold it by, and a press that hardly moves is still a click on a box; a pinch zooms about its middle; the keys + − 0 and the arrows work while the board or a box has the focus, and a box the keyboard reaches outside the view comes into it. The wheel zooms about the pointer with Ctrl or ⌘ held (a trackpad's pinch too), so a page that scrolls keeps its wheel, and one finger scrolls the page until the reader zooms in. The zoom goes from 10 % to 400 % of one pixel a scene unit, a large diagram down to half its fit, and a diagram can never be dragged out of sight. `<Diagram canvas>` makes the board fill its container, as a canvas the wheel alone zooms, with no Full screen button; `canvas={{ left: 420, right: 340 }}` names the pixels the page floats over it, and Fit frames the whole diagram in what they leave. Play starts from the reader's view and flies out to the whole diagram; a drag while the story plays stops it where it is, and it ends fitted. With reduced motion the buttons and the keys jump. The code that pans and zooms loads with the first board that does: a page without `zoom` loads nothing more. Pictures, posters, GIFs and videos show what the camera frames, never the reader's view, as before. Full screen's compact panels and a box's detail now load when the reader first asks for them, so a `<Loadmap>` page loads less than before. The editor's diagram pans and zooms.
