The scenarios and the load in one bar over the diagram, so the reader moves the load and watches what it does. `<Loadmap>` draws `LoadBar` where the scenarios were: it stays in view while the page scrolls (`--lm-sticky-top` on an ancestor keeps it under a page's own fixed header), and on a phone it sits under the diagram, in thumb reach. Full screen and focus put it in their head (focus shows the scenarios now, and its line keeps the verdict); on a phone, over the verdict. `LoadRange`, the slider itself, has a track coloured by what the load does (under the target, past it, past capacity, a ± range as a blend), a log scale when the load spans ten times or more, the date the growth reaches, and under it a mark for today, each preset that sets the load, the 50 % target and where it runs out, which a click takes; ← → move a step, Page Up and Page Down to the next mark. Its input now holds the place on the track, not the load: set the load with `setValues`. `Controls` takes `load={false}` to leave the load to the bar, and shows only the presets that set no load; full screen's Load card is Design, and goes when the model has nothing but the load. A `<Loadmap>` page loads 1.6 kB more; its budget is 51 kB.
